Optional rank-affine prompt dispatch

train.train_policy.data_dispatch_as_rank_in_mesh defaults to false. Normal shared-queue completion dispatch does not need it. When enabled, dataset indices select both the rollout replica and the destination policy replica by modulo their respective mesh sizes in disaggregated mode. A rollout replica can reach only policy replicas in its modulo-gcd(rollout_replicas, policy_replicas) class.

Reservation and replacement contract

In disaggregated non-DAPO training, prompt capacity is per requested weight version and destination policy replica, not just a global prompt count. The controller records destination affinity with the issued producer reservation. A returned payload cannot change that destination. Its reported generation version is not used to reconstruct either its original quota or its destination.

Terminal rejection reopens the affected destination’s prompt capacity. Partial losses accumulate per (requested_version, policy_rank) and are rounded up to whole n_generation groups there. Losses on two different policy queues must not share one replacement group: no prompt can replenish both queues. Report replay and producer retirement use the same original coordinates and cannot reopen a slot twice. Completed work releases its affinity metadata; trained versions release their fetch/refill bookkeeping. Retirement validates against the reserved mesh, not the number of still-live trainers: the last trainer can leave before rollout-owned slots are released.

The lazy fetcher applies destination quotas before pulling the dataset and retains the existing bounded scans and separate validation iterator. Global version assignment must not relabel its already sealed rank-local reservation.

Admission and bounded surplus

Only complete, balanced sets of policy queues advance the estimated training version. Staleness admission instead estimates each completion’s actual position in its own destination queue, including already reserved lookahead. Surplus on another queue must not make a needed completion appear one training step older. The dispatch-time staleness check remains authoritative.

Whole prompt groups may overfill a queue. For batch size B, group size G and P policy replicas, the ordinary group-rounded capacity is P * ceil(B / G) * G. Replenishment rounding adds fewer than G completions per destination per version. Rank-quota-controlled admission therefore adds P * (G - 1) headroom to the existing soft/hard sample ceilings, so bounded surplus cannot starve a short peer. Destination quotas still constrain fetching; this does not disable admission limits or increase the permitted staleness.

Colocated preparation owns its local usable-batch budget and can fetch replacement groups until ready or genuinely exhausted. Its rank-affine prompt fetches therefore retain the modulo shard mapping and bounded scans, but do not apply the disaggregated fixed prompt quota. A local rejection must not strand preparation at that quota. Colocated workers forward their rollout mesh rank just like disaggregated workers; paired policies consume that rollout shard locally rather than rerouting completions through global policy queues.

End of data

Foreign-rank spill rows do not suppress a source’s end-of-data receipt. Already buffered rows for another source remain available to that source. A known final iterator does not advance epochs repeatedly while sources drain those rows.

After the final training iterator ends, the existing serialized controller status tick checks each policy queue’s maximum possible remaining capacity: accepted rows plus sealed lookahead plus producer-owned slots plus n_generation * unissued prompts. If even this optimistic bound cannot fill one policy batch, future prompt input closes for every producer. Issued work and reports still drain normally; ordinary end-of-data completion preserves final validation and checkpointing at the last completed update. It does not invent samples, optimizer updates, or successful recovery of a lost trainer cohort.

Default shared-queue behavior and DAPO, SFT and validation fetch budgets retain their existing policies. This does not add elastic mesh recovery, change advantages or losses, or qualify any particular payload transport.

Regression validation

tests/test_rank_dispatch_refill.py covers rectangular meshes, rank-local partial-loss rounding, unchanged HTTP retry semantics, both identified and ordinary reports, reservation affinity, producer retirement and staleness admission, source-local EOF and conservative partial-tail input closure. tests/rank_dispatch_canary.py runs two real collective optimizer participants on batches admitted after injected partial failures:

torchrun --standalone --nproc-per-node=2 tests/rank_dispatch_canary.py --backend gloo
torchrun --standalone --nproc-per-node=2 tests/rank_dispatch_canary.py --backend nccl

The canary uses synthetic generation and disables automatic command publication; it validates controller accounting plus collective progress, not end-to-end rollout payload transfer or production throughput.
